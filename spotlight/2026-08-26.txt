[TITLE] CLEAR Accepted at EMNLP 2026 Main Conference
[TAG] Research
[PREVIEW] Dissecting Transformers: A CLEAR Perspective Towards Green AI has been accepted at EMNLP 2026, establishing where energy is actually consumed inside Transformer architectures.
[CONTENT] Our work 𝗗𝗶𝘀𝘀𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗧𝗿𝗮𝗻𝘀𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗺𝗲𝗿𝘀: 𝗔 𝗖𝗟𝗘𝗔𝗥 𝗣𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗧𝗼𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗱𝘀 𝗚𝗿𝗲𝗲𝗻 𝗔𝗜 has been accepted as a main conference paper at EMNLP 2026.

Most Green AI studies measure energy at the level of the whole model. This work goes a level deeper, to establish where the energy inside Transformer architectures is actually going.

𝗠𝗲𝘁𝗵𝗼𝗱𝗼𝗹𝗼𝗴𝘆. CLEAR stands for Component-Level Energy Assessment via Repetitions. Transformer components often execute in microseconds, while GPU energy sensors operate on a far coarser timescale. CLEAR closes that gap by repeatedly executing individual components, including Attention, MLP, normalisation and the LM Head, to obtain reliable component-level energy measurements. Across 15 models spanning four Transformer architecture families, CLEAR captures more than 90% of total model energy.

𝗞𝗲𝘆 𝗳𝗶𝗻𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴𝘀. Attention has the highest energy per FLOP among the components studied. The KV cache matters for energy as well as for speed, reducing Attention energy by more than 60% at longer contexts. FLOPs alone are not sufficient to characterise energy consumption, since components performing similar amounts of computation can carry very different energy costs. Different Attention implementations and optimisations also lead to substantial differences in energy consumed.

![Component-level energy analysis with CLEAR](/images/spotlight/CLEAR2.png)

𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗻𝗲𝘅𝘁. The results open the way to guidelines that let practitioners reason about energy while designing models, rather than only measuring it afterwards. There is also scope to extend this fine-grained perspective from inference to training and model adaptation, and to build towards a theory for estimating energy directly from neural architectures.

The work is by Hemang Jain, Shailender Goyal and Divyansh Pandey, all undergraduate research students, and represents more than a year of sustained effort, including a close call at ICLR and a substantial rework before this acceptance.

This research is supported by the Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF) PMECRG grant under the SustAInd project.
