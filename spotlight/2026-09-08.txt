![Best Reviewer Award at ECSA 2026](/images/spotlight/best_reviewerecsa.png)

The talk took up a question that sits at the centre of current work in the area: what happens when the mechanisms responsible for adaptation themselves become agentic. Tracing how the self-adaptation community has treated uncertainty, runtime reasoning and learning over the years, and drawing on recent SA4S work on agentic self-adaptation and uncertainty in GenAI-based systems, it set out a possible progression from engineered rules, to learning, to reasoning, and then to whatever follows.

Agentic AI provides new machinery for reasoning about and potentially generating adaptations at runtime. It also introduces a paradox. Agents are brought in to manage uncertainty, yet the agents themselves become new sources of uncertainty, along with new interactions between those sources. The keynote closed on the architectural question this raises: should autonomy itself become an adaptation variable?
